01 · The rhythm of the work

Two days did six runs in ten. And nine in ten built our own machine.

The seat works in bursts: Jul 11–12 alone logged 440 runs — 59.6% of the daily series — then it settled to about 37 a day. Of everything it did, 91.0% was internal system work; client-facing runs were 66, spread across five named clients.

02 · Where the work went

Code does the volume. Design does the weight.

86.4% of the month’s runs (636 of 736) were multi-file code edits — quick jobs, typically 2.3 minutes. A design build is the opposite: typically 24.7 minutes, and 41.5 at the 90th percentile, chewing 363,572 tokens per typical run — 7.3× a code task’s.

04 · The punchline

Two kinds of capacity. Two different levers.

Flat-rate seat · prepaid capacity
The lever is utilization.

A flat seat costs the same whether it runs 7 tasks or 736. Ten days of production work moved the bill by $0.00 — and 96.7% of it ran at the model’s highest effort setting. Every idle hour is capacity you already paid for, going to waste. Feed the seat.

JUL 11–20 · 736 runs · $0.00 marginal each · 96.7% xhigh
Metered API · elastic capacity
The lever is quality per dollar.

On the meter, spend follows value token by token. $3.17 bought six authored pages, three blind scorecards, and two judged bake-offs — one loss and one win. Fund small, judge the output blind, then scale only what wins.

05 · Acting on it

Three moves for next month.

Keep code on the flat seat.

86.4% of all runs are multi-file code edits — typically 2.3 minutes at $0.00 marginal. Prepaid capacity is doing exactly what it was bought for; don’t route that volume anywhere priced per token.

Point the seat at design next.

Design builds are the heaviest work on the books — typically 24.7 min, 41.5 on slow days, 363,572 tokens a run — yet only 5 ran all month. On a flat plan every extra one is free at the margin. That’s unused leverage.

Buy verdicts on the meter.

Two blind bake-offs cost $1.19 and $1.99 — and returned opposite verdicts: Kimi last on client-brand briefs, winner of the open wow round. With $21.83 still unspent, small metered bets stay the cheapest way to judge a model.
